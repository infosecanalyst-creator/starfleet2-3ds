# Getting started
New to Star Fleet II? This page walks you through your first ten minutes. The game is a real-time starship simulation: you command a Krellan battlecruiser, and the clock keeps running while you read. L (FREEZE) stops the clock at any time; press it again to go on.

1. Title screens. Tap CONTINUE (or press A) to move past the title art.

2. Sign on. The keyboard opens by itself. Type a name for your officer and tap ENTER. When the game asks whether you are a new recruit, tap YES, then type a password you will remember and tap ENTER. Next time, SIGN ON AS <NAME> does all of this in one tap.

3. Sign-on options. This menu becomes big buttons on the touch screen. For your first time, choose Practice: it is a safe place to learn the bridge. Start a new mission when you are ready for the real thing.

4. Your orders. Before you fly, the game shows your orders and other pages of text. The touch screen shows the text in large letters: drag it with the stylus or use either stick to scroll; tap CONTINUE or press A to go on. During a mission the ORDERS tab brings your orders back at any time.

5. The bridge. The touch screen is your helm: the tactical display (your ship is in the middle), NAV and the action buttons. The top screen is what you watch: alert lights, COMMS messages, ENERGY, the long-range scan (LRS), DAMAGE and DATA.
- Steer: tap the tactical display where you want to go, or push the Circle Pad left or right.
- Speed: Circle Pad up or down for sublight speed; C-Stick up or down for hyperspace speed (for long trips between stars).
- Fight: X fires phasers, Y fires torpedoes, SHLD+ raises the shields.
- Look around: STARS, SYSTEM and WAR are the maps; COMBAT, FLEET, ORDERS and LOG are the other displays. B (Esc) backs out of a display.

6. Questions. When the game asks something, the line just above the buttons shows the question and the buttons become the answers. When nothing is asked, that line says what to do on this screen (the top screen's gold strip says it too).

7. Saving. The game saves a mission under a name you give it (its Save Mission command); after that, SELECT (or START, then QUICK SAVE) on the bridge saves again under that name (the game's Ctrl+Q). When you leave with START, then EXIT GAME, while on the bridge, the port tries the game's own save first and the touch screen says "Saving..." until a save file is written: it uses Ctrl+Q, and if the mission has no save name yet it uses the full save and names it EXITSAVE (answering yes if EXITSAVE already exists). Closing the game from the HOME Menu does the same (as far as the 3DS allows). Practice missions may not be savable: if the game says so, or nothing is saved, EXIT GAME asks "Not saved! Exit anyway?" (NO is ringed; NO keeps playing). Away from the bridge EXIT GAME warns that anything since your last save is lost. Pressing HOME by itself only pauses the game; it does not save.

8. Learning more. The Training Manual tutorials further down this manual (Tutorial 1 onward) teach flying, combat and missions step by step: follow them in Practice. The Keyboard Quick Reference lists the game's own keys; you never need them, because the port presses them for you and the row of buttons under the prompt shows the game's current commands.

Lost? Press START and choose MANUAL: Getting started is the first section.

# Playing on the 3DS
Star Fleet II runs unchanged inside DOSBox. This port reads the game's screen and redraws it across both 3DS screens, so you never need a keyboard.

The top screen shows everything you watch: alert lights, communications, energy, long-range scan, damage, data (with your speeds: s: sublight, c: hyperspace), mission time and status. On the bridge its gold strip along the bottom lists the main buttons.

The touch screen shows everything you touch: the tactical display, navigation, action buttons, the game's own command menu and the view tabs.

Graphics screens (the title art) appear on the top screen as the game drew them. Tap CONTINUE or press A to go on.

# Touch screen
TACTICAL DISPLAY: tap anywhere to set a course toward that point. The port works out the bearing and turns the ship for you. When the game shows its target box (Weapons targeting set to Manual), a tap moves the box to the ship or object you touched instead; tap it again to lock on (Enter).

AUTO / STEER / TARGET: the button under NAV (or R) chooses what a tactical tap does. AUTO, the default, targets when the target box is up and steers otherwise. STEER always steers; TARGET always moves the box. Your choice is remembered next time you play.

ACTION BUTTONS (right column): PHASERS, TORPS, SHLD+ / SHLD- (5% steps), SPD+ / SPD- (sublight 0.1c steps), the tap mode (AUTO, STEER or TARGET) and FREEZE (pause or resume the simulation).

PROMPT LINE: shows what the game is asking. Yes/no questions get YES and NO buttons; "press any key" gets CONTINUE. When the game is not asking anything, the line says (in pale blue) what to do on this screen; the gold strip at the bottom of the top screen says the same.

ALERTS: in flight, when one of the alert lights on the top screen comes on (LOW PWR, HULL PN and the others), the prompt line shows it for 3 seconds in a red box ("ALERT: HULL PN"); a new COMMS message shows the same way in a gold box ("MSG: ..."), so you never have to look up to notice them.

COMMAND MENU: the row of buttons under the prompt is the game's own menu, read live from the screen. The red letter is the item's hotkey; the highlighted button is the current item. When the menu has more items than fit in one row, MORE at the right end shows the next ones.

VIEW TABS: STARS, SYSTEM, WAR, COMBAT, FLEET, ORDERS and LOG jump straight to those displays (they work during a mission; outside one they are grey). The first two times you tap each one, the prompt line says what it is:
- STARS: the star map; tap a star to aim at it.
- SYSTEM: the map of the star system you are in.
- WAR: the whole war at a glance.
- COMBAT: combat control, orders for your ships.
- FLEET: the state of your ships.
- ORDERS: your mission orders again.
- LOG: the messages so far.
This manual is in the START menu (MANUAL).

OTHER DISPLAYS: on a menu screen the menu becomes big buttons on the touch screen (tap one to choose it) and the rest of the screen stays on top; a menu too long for the touch screen shows five rows at a time: drag the list or tap the arrow buttons beside it (the bar between shows where you are). On a map (star, system and war maps, combat control) the map goes on the touch screen: tap a star or ship to move the target box there, and tap it again to select. Nothing is shown on both screens.

TEXT PAGES (orders, reports, briefings): the touch screen shows the page's text in large letters, joined into paragraphs. Drag it with the stylus, tap the up/down arrow buttons under it, or use the sticks: up/down scroll the text, and at its end they page the game's own text (left/right page up/down). The top screen does not show the page again: it shows the page's title, which lines you are reading ("Lines 12-22 of 40") with a bar for where you are in the page, and the controls in its gold strip. Tapping a word with a red hotkey letter presses that key. The row under the text keeps the game's own keys: PG UP, the four arrows, PG DN and SPACE. Flight windows keep a larger pad of those keys instead.

KEYBOARD: opens by itself when the game wants typed text (your name, password, notepad). The KEYBOARD button opens it any time (SELECT does too, away from the bridge).

QUESTIONS: any yes/no question the game asks (such as "Do you want to switch to Practice mode?") gets YES and NO buttons, even over a menu.

SIGN-ON: after you sign on once, the port remembers your officer. Next time, tap SIGN ON AS <NAME> under the keyboard and it enters your name and password for you. The officer is kept in /3ds/starfleet2/officer.txt; delete that file to forget it.

Every touch button clicks and visibly presses in when you tap it.

BUTTON RING (ZR): everything you can tap also works without the stylus. Press ZR and a gold ring appears on a touch button; the D-Pad moves it to the next button in that direction (grey buttons are skipped), A presses the ringed button, and B or ZR hands the D-Pad and A back to the game. The bridge's gold strip on the top screen reminds you: "ZR buttons".

SETTINGS KEPT: the tap mode, the manual section you last read, left-handed play and the screen swap are kept in /3ds/starfleet2/ (settings.txt, lefty.txt, .swapped) and come back the next time you play.

# Using this manual
Circle Pad or C-Stick: scroll the text (hold to keep scrolling). You can also drag the stylus up or down on the touch screen.
D-Pad: move between the section buttons on the touch screen. Past the last section it moves on to LEFT HAND and CLOSE MANUAL (a gold ring shows which); A presses the ringed button.
L / R: previous / next section.
Tap a section on the touch screen to open it. When there are more sections than fit, the arrow buttons beside the list page it, and the bar between them shows where you are.
B or START: close the manual.
The manual opens again on the section you were reading when you closed it, even after you quit and restart the game.

# Left-handed play
LEFT HAND (the button at the bottom of the manual's touch screen) swaps the D-Pad with the face buttons: Y and Up, A and Right, B and Down, X and Left. So the D-Pad becomes Enter, Esc, phasers and torpedoes, and the face buttons become the arrow keys. The setting is saved in /3ds/starfleet2/lefty.txt and also applies to this manual.
